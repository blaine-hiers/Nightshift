## What the reviews caught

Fifteen of the 16 review passes (14 per-ticket, 1 integration, 1 re-review) found
something. Highlights:

- **#11**: about 12 of 24 GPU TFLOPS values were FP32 shader rates, not dense
  tensor rates, which would make TTFT read 2–4x too fast. The coordinator then
  caught 2 more in the corrected table: RTX 6000 Ada and RTX PRO 6000
  Blackwell, workstation cards that do FP32 accumulate at full rate.
- **#5**: the generated `llama-server -hf <id>` could never run on the
  safetensors presets, and model ids went into a shell command unquoted.
- **#7 and #17**: each feature's capacity figures (max users, context table,
  chart) contradicted its own fit badge. This led to the shared
  `fixedBytes`/`bytesPerUser` contract.
- **#18**: offline navigation worked only for URLs already visited. The
  reviewer found it by actually driving Chromium offline, and the fix found a
  second bug (`Vary: Origin` defeating `caches.match`).
- **Integration review, 6 cross-feature bugs** that no per-ticket review could
  see: cost ignored offload and speculation; the launch command's `-ngl` and
  `--cpu-offload-gb` disagreed with the verdict; offload capacity just echoed
  the inputs; `fits` ignored offload in the matrix and compare; the GGUF picker
  leaked across compare columns; the service worker cached non-HTML as the shell.
- **Re-review**: the fix round had introduced `-ngl <numLayers>` in place of 999
  when nothing spills. That leaves llama.cpp's output layer on the CPU.
